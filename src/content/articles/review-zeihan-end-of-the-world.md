## Review Overview & Core Thesis

This published review analyzes Peter Zeihan's 2022 geopolitical forecast, which
predicts the impending collapse of the US-enforced, post-WWII globalized trading
system. The review evaluates Zeihan's core argument: as the United States retreats
from its role as the global security guarantor, nations will be forced to abandon
global competition and restructure to independently secure domestic food,
manufacturing, and energy. The review highlights Zeihan's focus on geography and
demographic collapse — specifically rapidly aging and shrinking populations — as
the primary drivers of this deglobalization.

## Structural Analysis of the Text

The review systematically breaks down Zeihan's seven-part framework, tracing the
transition from historical integration to future localized survival:

- **The Breakdown of 'The Order' (Sections I–III):** Examines the historical
  establishment of US global hegemony and the demographic shifts making current
  transport, maritime trade, and global financial models unsustainable.
- **Resource Dependency (Sections IV–V):** Analyzes the modern reliance on
  US-managed supply chains for fossil fuels and industrial materials, noting that
  the transition to "Greentech" is structurally hindered by geographic and
  infrastructural limitations.
- **Production & Survival (Sections VI–VII):** Evaluates Zeihan's predictions
  regarding mass production and agriculture, emphasizing that complex supply
  chains will fracture. Success will depend on regional connectivity, while the
  breakdown of specialized agricultural transport threatens severe global food
  insecurity.

## Conclusion & Strategic Outlook

The review concludes by assessing Zeihan's geopolitical forecast: the end of
globalization does not signal a return to pre-industrialism, but rather a harsh
recalibration where survival is dictated by localized geography, transport, and
demographics. The analysis notes that Zeihan envisions a gradual systemic decay —
ending "not with a bang but a whimper" — driven by protectionist policies and
systemic withdrawals, inevitably triggering inflation and disrupted supply chains
on a global scale.
