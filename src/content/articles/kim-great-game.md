## Research Overview

This recently published 6,500-word study explores the function of "geopolitical
tropes" — figures of speech operating at the intersection of geography and
political power. It argues that these tropes serve as cognitive shortcuts,
compressing complex, multilateral dynamics into zero-sum binaries that make
global affairs accessible to the public while often obscuring ground realities.
By tracing the evolution of "The Great Game" from a 19th-century imperial rivalry
to a contemporary descriptor for superpower competition, the article highlights
literature's foundational role in manufacturing geopolitical consent.

## Theoretical Framework

The study applies a dual analytical framework to dissect how popular culture
actively influences foreign policy:

- **Critical Geopolitics:** Utilized for macro-level analysis, exposing how
  geography is politically inscribed and mapped by the powerful to justify
  statecraft and foreign interventions.
- **Narrative Theory:** Applied as a micro-level tool to decode the technical
  architecture of storytelling (such as plot, pacing, and character archetypes)
  to understand how these fictions successfully persuade mass audiences.

## Key Findings: The Case of Kim (1901)

- **Literature as Narrative Infrastructure:** The analysis reveals that Kipling's
  text operates not as neutral entertainment, but as active narrative
  infrastructure. By centering on a hybrid protagonist whose local expertise
  serves British strategic interests, the novel normalizes the Anglo-Russian
  rivalry as a heroic quest rather than raw imperial strife.
- **Constructing Civilizational Binaries:** The novel engineers a spatial
  hierarchy where India is depicted as fragmented and irrational (justifying
  British rule as a necessary ordering force), while Russia is cast as a
  tyrannical "Other" (validating British military expansionism as a purely
  defensive measure).
- **Geopolitical Enculturation:** The immense popularity of Kim implanted
  long-lasting imperial tropes into the collective consciousness, ultimately
  providing British policymakers with a cultural framework to justify military
  spending and regional interventions.

## Contemporary Relevance: Popular Geopolitics 2.0

The study concludes by bridging historical literary analysis with the modern
digital age. Today, the boundaries between elite discourse and popular
understanding are highly porous. As the world navigates a period of
deglobalization and renewed superpower rivalries, dominant paradigms like "Great
Power Competition" and the "Thucydides Trap" function exactly as "The Great Game"
did. The research demonstrates that geopolitical tropes remain a powerful,
double-edged sword capable of rapidly shifting public sentiment and escalating
global tensions in an era of accelerated media consumption.
