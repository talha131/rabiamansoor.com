## Review Overview & Core Thesis

This published review evaluates Mai'a K. Davis Cross's 2017 analysis of European
integration and institutional resilience. The review highlights Cross's central
argument: rather than signaling the collapse of the EU, structural crises trigger
a state of "integrational panic" — amplified by media and political elites — that
ultimately serves as a catalyst for deeper integration. The review unpacks
Cross's focus on the power of "social narratives" and her tripartite analytical
framework (systemic, behavioral, and sociological) to explain how public
sentiment is actively shaped during periods of instability.

## The Anatomy of Crisis & Case Studies

The review systematically breaks down Cross's predictable five-stage crisis model
(Trigger, Buildup, Crisis Height, Catharsis, Resolution) by analyzing three major
21st-century European events:

- **2003 EU Iraq Crisis:** Examines the initial fragmentation of the newly formed
  Common Foreign and Security Policy (CFSP) under post-9/11 US pressure, which
  ultimately resolved into a deeper, unified defense and security strategy.
- **2005 Constitutional Treaty Crisis:** Analyzes the severe panic following the
  French and Dutch "no" votes, demonstrating how this democratic rejection
  ultimately paved a smooth path for the passage of the 2009 Lisbon Treaty.
- **2010 Eurozone Crisis:** Highlights how the localized economic downturn in
  Greece (accounting for only 2% of the EU economy) was weaponized and magnified
  by elite narratives into an existential threat regarding the creditor North and
  debtor South.

## Conclusion & Academic Significance

The review concludes that Cross's work provides a vital, thought-provoking
counter-perspective to traditional political science. By demonstrating how media
and elites politicize socially driven issues to manufacture existential panics,
the book successfully dismantles popular "Doomsday scenarios" and offers a
resilient, highly structural model of European political development.
